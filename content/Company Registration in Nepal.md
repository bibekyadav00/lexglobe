## Introduction
Online company registration in Nepal is the process of legally incorporating a business entity through the electronic system of the Office of the Company Registrar (OCR), operating under the Ministry of Industry, Commerce and Supplies. The online registration platform replaced the paper-based filing method, allowing entrepreneurs to submit documents, reserve names, and receive registration certificates digitally. This process is governed by the Companies Act, 2063 (2006) and corresponding regulations. The system enhances efficiency, transparency, and accessibility for both domestic and foreign investors. Applicants can complete all formalities—from name approval to certificate issuance—through the OCR portal. Online registration ensures that all corporate data is maintained in digital records, minimizing physical paperwork. It also allows business owners to update information and file annual returns online, aligning corporate compliance with modern governance standards.

## Legal Framework under the Companies Act, 2063 (2006)
The legal foundation for online company registration in Nepal is set by the Companies Act, 2063 (2006), its amendments, and subsidiary regulations. The Act outlines the incorporation, operation, management, and dissolution of companies. It applies to all forms of companies registered within Nepal, including private, public, and non-profit entities. Section 3 of the Act mandates that any person intending to undertake business activities must register as a company with the Office of the Company Registrar. The Companies Regulation, 2064, provides detailed procedural requirements for digital submissions and prescribed document formats. The Act also defines provisions on share capital, directorship, annual filings, mergers, and penalties for non-compliance. Through this legislation, Nepal’s company law aligns with international corporate governance principles, emphasizing transparency, accountability, and legal protection for shareholders and creditors.

## Types of Companies in Nepal
The Companies Act, 2063 (2006) recognizes three main categories of companies based on ownership and purpose:

a. Private Limited Company – Formed by at least one and up to 101 shareholders. Its shares are not transferable to the general public.

b. Public Limited Company – Requires at least seven shareholders and can issue shares to the public after obtaining approval from regulatory bodies.

c. Company Not Distributing Profits (Non-Profit Company) – Registered under Section 166(2), it operates for social, educational, charitable, or professional objectives without profit distribution.

In addition, foreign companies can register branch or liaison offices in Nepal under Sections 154 and 155. The selection of company type depends on the business purpose, capital structure, and operational model. Each type carries distinct compliance obligations, registration fees, and reporting requirements.

## Eligibility Criteria for Company Registration in Nepal
To register a company in Nepal, applicants must fulfill basic eligibility conditions prescribed by the Companies Act, 2063 (2006) and related directives.

- For private limited companies, a minimum of one (1) shareholder and one director is required. The same person may serve in both roles.

- For public limited companies, there must be at least seven (7)shareholders and three directors.

- For foreign companies, a branch or liaison office may be registered after obtaining approval from the relevant authority and submitting a notarized copy of incorporation documents from the parent country.

All promoters must be natural or legal persons legally capable of entering into a contract under Nepali law.

The registered office must be located within Nepal, and a local address is mandatory for communication.

The business name must not conflict with existing registered names or restricted terms.

## Documents Required for Online Company Registration in Nepal
Applicants must prepare and upload the following documents to complete online company registration through the OCR portal:

- Memorandum of Association (MOA)

- Articles of Association (AOA)

- Copy of citizenship certificate(s) or passport (for foreign promoters)

- Lease agreement or ownership document of the registered office address

- Power of attorney (if applicable)

- Consent letter from proposed directors

- Company name approval letter (received from OCR system)

- Tax registration (PAN) application form (post-registration stage)

All documents must be digitally signed and submitted in the prescribed format. Scanned copies must be legible, and information across documents must be consistent with promoter details.

## Step-by-Step Process for Online Company Registration in Nepal
The online company registration process in Nepal follows the steps outlined below:

- Name Reservation – Apply for name approval through the OCR portal. Once approved, the name remains valid for 35 days.

- Prepare Documents – Draft the MOA, AOA, and other required forms according to company type.

- Online Application Submission – Log in to the OCR system, fill in company details, upload scanned documents, and submit.

- Verification by OCR – The registrar reviews the application and may request clarifications or corrections.

- Payment of Registration Fees – Pay the applicable registration fee as per the authorized capital.

- Issuance of Certificate – Upon approval, OCR issues the digital Certificate of Incorporation.

- Post-Registration Steps – Apply for PAN, VAT (if applicable), and register with local authorities.

Once approved, the company becomes a separate legal entity capable of entering contracts and owning assets in its own name.

## Online Company Registration Portal (OCR Nepal)
The Office of the Company Registrar (OCR) provides a secure web-based system for company registration, renewal, and compliance submissions. The portal allows users to perform end-to-end incorporation tasks, such as reserving names, submitting forms, and downloading certificates. It supports both Nepali and English interfaces for user convenience. The OCR database maintains a public registry of all registered entities, accessible for verification. Users must create an account on the OCR platform to initiate the process. Digital submission eliminates the need for physical presence, reducing processing time and improving transparency. OCR staff review documents electronically and communicate feedback through the portal. Payments for registration and renewal can be made via bank vouchers or electronic banking channels. The portal aligns with the government’s digital transformation initiative to promote a paperless corporate system.

## Post-Incorporation Compliance in Nepal
Once your company is successfully incorporated and you’ve received the Certificate of Incorporation from the Office of the Company Registrar (OCR), the process does not end there. A newly registered company in Nepal must complete several post-registration compliance tasks before it can legally operate or open a bank account. These are essential for taxation, record-keeping, and operational authorization.

1. Permanent Account Number (PAN) Registration

The first compliance requirement after incorporation is obtaining a Permanent Account Number (PAN) from the Inland Revenue Department (IRD). The PAN serves as a tax identification number for both corporate and individual taxpayers in Nepal.

All registered companies must apply for PAN within 30 days of incorporation.

The application is filed online through the IRD portal, and the printed acknowledgment must be verified at the nearest Tax Office.

Without a PAN, the company cannot open a corporate bank account or issue tax invoices.

Documents required for PAN registration include:

- Certificate of Incorporation

- Memorandum and Articles of Association

- Office registration proof (rental agreement or ownership certificate)

- Company seal and letterhead

- Director’s citizenship certificate copy

2. Value Added Tax (VAT) Registration

If a company’s annual turnover is expected to exceed Rs. 5 million, VAT registration is mandatory under the Value Added Tax Act, 2052 (1996).

VAT registration is done at the same tax office where PAN was obtained.

Once registered, the company must issue tax invoices bearing its VAT number and collect 13% VAT on taxable goods or services.

VAT returns must be filed monthly within the 25th of the next month.

Non-compliance with VAT registration or delayed filings can result in penalties and interest charges. Therefore, it is important to plan for timely accounting and compliance from the start.

3. Share Certificate Issuance
Within two months from the date of incorporation, the company must issue share certificates to its shareholders.
Each certificate should include:

- Company name and registration number

- Number of shares allotted to the shareholder

- Paid-up capital value per share

- Signature of the managing director or authorized person

Failure to issue share certificates in time can attract penalties and complicate record verification during audits or capital increases.

4. Minute Book and Statutory Records
Every company must maintain:

- A Minute Book recording the proceedings of meetings.

- A Register of Members, showing shareholder names, shareholding patterns, and transfers.

- A Register of Directors with personal details, appointment dates, and tenure.

- A Register of Charges, showing any encumbrances on company assets.

These records must be kept at the registered office and made available to shareholders or inspectors upon request.

5. Company Bank Account Opening
A company can open a bank account only after obtaining its PAN certificate. Banks generally require:

- Certificate of Incorporation

- PAN certificate

- Shareholder list

- Board Resolution authorizing account opening

- Identification documents of authorized signatories

Foreign investors must also provide approval from the Department of Industry (DOI) or the Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) for capital inflow, depending on the investment category.

6. Annual Filings with OCR
Every registered company in Nepal is legally obligated to submit annual returns to the Office of the Company Registrar.
The annual return includes:

- Financial statements (audited)

- Details of directors and shareholders

- Confirmation of registered address and paid-up capital

Filing must be completed within six months after the end of the fiscal year (i.e., by Poush end). Failure to file attracts fines as per Section 81 of the Companies Act, as shown in the schedule earlier.

7. Audit and Accounting Requirements
Under the Companies Act 2063 and Income Tax Act 2058, every company is required to maintain proper books of account and have them audited annually by a licensed Chartered Accountant (CA).
Audited financial statements must be submitted both to the OCR and the IRD.

For smaller companies, internal audits and simplified bookkeeping are allowed, but transparency and accuracy remain mandatory.

## Registration of a Foreign Company in Nepal
A foreign company wishing to conduct business in Nepal must register either as a Branch Office or a Liaison Office, depending on its intended activities.

1. Branch Office Registration

A Branch Office allows a foreign company to carry out commercial operations, earn revenue, and pay taxes in Nepal.
The process is governed by Section 154 of the Companies Act 2063, and registration is handled by the Office of the Company Registrar (OCR).

Documents Required:

- Certified copy of Certificate of Incorporation from home country

- Memorandum and Articles of Association (translated into English or Nepali)

- Board Resolution authorizing the branch setup

- Power of Attorney appointing a local representative in Nepal

- Details of the principal place of business in Nepal

- Approval from the Department of Industry (DOI) if applicable

Fees:

As shown earlier in the official fee schedule, the registration fee for a branch office ranges from Rs. 15,000 to Rs. 160,000, depending on the level of proposed investment.

Once approved, the branch is issued a Branch Registration Certificate, after which it must:

- Obtain PAN and VAT from the Inland Revenue Department

- Maintain local accounting and reporting as per Nepali standards

- Submit annual statements to OCR, including profit/loss details of its operations in Nepal

2. Liaison or Contact Office Registration
A Liaison Office is a non-commercial representation of a foreign company in Nepal. It cannot generate revenue or undertake commercial transactions.
Its role is limited to communication, information exchange, market research, and coordination with local agencies.

Registration Process:

- Apply to the Department of Industry (DOI) for permission.

- Register with the Office of the Company Registrar (OCR) as a liaison office.

- Pay a one-time registration fee of Rs. 50,000.

Liaison offices must also obtain PAN for administrative expenses but are not required to register for VAT or pay corporate income tax.

3. Taxation of Foreign Companies
Foreign companies operating in Nepal are taxed based on their income sourced within Nepal.

- The corporate tax rate for general business activities is 25%.

- For projects involving technology transfer or double taxation agreements (DTAs), rates may vary.

- Branch profits are also subject to repatriation approval by Nepal Rastra Bank.

## Common Issues During Online Company Registration
Despite the digital system introduced by the OCR, applicants often face challenges during the online registration process. These issues can lead to application delays or rejections.


1. Rejection of Company Name

The OCR is strict about name similarity and duplication.
A company name that closely resembles an existing one, includes restricted words (like “National,” “Government,” or “Bank”), or lacks legal structure (e.g., Pvt. Ltd.) is likely to be rejected.
It’s recommended to search the company name database before submission.


2. Mismatch in Documents
Applications are commonly rejected due to discrepancies between the uploaded and printed documents — especially in:

- Director names or citizenship numbers

- Capital structure

- Share ratios

- Signature mismatches

It is advisable to double-check all digital documents before final submission.


3. Non-Compliance with OCR Format
The OCR has specific templates for:

- Memorandum of Association (MOA)

- Articles of Association (AOA)

- Declaration of Compliance

Any deviation from the prescribed format may result in rejection or queries. It is best to prepare these documents through legal professionals familiar with OCR formatting requirements.


4. Technical Issues in the OCR Portal
Applicants occasionally face slow uploads or system errors, especially during peak submission periods or fiscal year-end.
In such cases:

- Save your draft regularly.

- Use supported file formats (PDF under 2MB).

- Access the system during off-peak hours.


5. Delay in Approval
The average approval timeline for a new company is 3 to 5 working days if all documents are in order. However, complex ownership structures or foreign shareholders may extend processing times.

To avoid delay:

- Provide complete KYC of all directors/shareholders.

- Ensure accurate translations of foreign documents.

- Use consistent email addresses for communication.

## Legal Provisions Governing Company Registration
The Companies Act, 2063 (2006) is the primary legislation that regulates company formation, operation, and dissolution in Nepal. The Act is supported by subsidiary regulations, such as the Company Rules, 2064, and directives issued by the Office of the Company Registrar (OCR).

Key Legal Sections:

- Section 3 – Definition of Company: Defines a company as an entity incorporated under this Act with separate legal existence.

- Section 4 – Types of Companies: Classifies companies into private, public, and non-profit categories.

- Section 5 – Requirement for Registration: States that no business can operate as a company unless registered with the OCR.

- Section 13 – Conversion of Company Type: Allows a company to convert from private to public and vice versa.

- Section 18 – Registration Procedure: Outlines application submission, document review, and issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation.

- Section 51 onwards – Compliance and Reporting: Mandates companies to maintain updated records and file annual reports.

The Companies Act provides the foundation for corporate governance, ensuring accountability, transparency, and protection of stakeholder interests.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What is online company registration in Nepal?
Online company registration is the process of forming a business through the Office of the Company Registrar (OCR) portal without visiting the office physically. Applicants can reserve names, upload documents, pay fees, and receive the certificate digitally.

2. Which law governs company registration in Nepal?
Company registration is regulated by the Companies Act 2063 (2006). It defines procedures, rights, and obligations of companies registered in Nepal.

3. What types of companies can be registered online?
Private Limited, Public Limited, and Non-Profit Companies can all be registered online through the OCR system, following the provisions under Section 4 of the Companies Act 2063.

4. How long does it take to register a company in Nepal?
If all documents are correct, the online registration process takes around 3 to 5 working days. Applications with foreign shareholders may take longer due to additional approvals.

5. What are the minimum shareholders required for registration?
A Private Limited Company requires at least one shareholder (single-shareholder structure allowed), while a Public Limited Company needs at least seven shareholders.

6. Can a foreigner register a company in Nepal?
Yes. Foreigners can register companies with investment approval from the Department of Industry (DOI) or through a Branch or Liaison Office under Section 154 of the Companies Act.

7. What is the registration fee for a private company?
The fee depends on authorized capital. For example, a company with up to Rs. 1,00,000 capital pays Rs. 1,000, and it increases progressively as per the official Schedule of Fees outlined in the Gazette.

8. What documents are required for registration?

- Memorandum and Articles of Association

- Citizenship of directors and shareholders

- Address verification

- Power of attorney (if applicable)

- Digital signature for e-filing

9. Is office space mandatory for registration?
Yes. Every company must declare a registered office address in Nepal and provide ownership documents or a lease agreement during registration.

10. When should a company apply for PAN and VAT?
A company must apply for PAN within 30 days of incorporation. VAT registration becomes mandatory when annual turnover exceeds Rs. 5 million.

11. What is the validity period of a company registration certificate?
The certificate of incorporation has no expiry date, but the company must maintain compliance through annual filings and updates.

12. What are the penalties for late filing?
Penalties range from Rs. 1,000 to Rs. 20,000 per year, depending on capital and duration of delay, as listed in Section (H) of the official fee schedule.

13. Can a company change its name after registration?
Yes. Under Section 21(3) of the Companies Act 2063, companies can apply for a name change by paying 25% of the registration fee or up to Rs. 5,000.

14. How can foreign investors repatriate profits?
Repatriation of profits is allowed under Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) approval once applicable taxes and compliances are completed.

15. Are digital signatures legally recognized?
Yes. Digital signatures issued by authorized certification providers are valid under Nepal’s Electronic Transaction Act 2063 (2008).

16. Can a company be registered without a lawyer?
Yes, but professional assistance ensures that MOA and AOA comply with OCR format and legal requirements, minimizing rejection risks.

17. What is the process to increase authorized capital?
The company must pass a Special Resolution and pay additional fees according to Section 56(1) of the Companies Act 2063.

18. What is a Non-Profit Company in Nepal?
A Non-Profit Company under Section 166(2) operates for social or public welfare without distributing profits to members. It pays a flat registration fee of Rs. 15,000.

19. Can an existing foreign company register a branch in Nepal?
Yes. Under Section 154, a foreign company can open a branch office after submitting its charter, board resolution, and financial statements to the OCR.
